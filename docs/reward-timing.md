# Reward timing

The current prelaunch ROBIN policy uses a 300-second holding requirement measured against finalized history and targets funded reward rounds every 900 seconds. Version 4 adds the explicitly reviewed `pipelined-finalized-v1` confirmation policy with at most eight transactions awaiting finality. The immutable contract source and fee split remain unchanged.

Each acquisition matures independently after five minutes on the finalized chain timeline. This is not five minutes of wall-clock time after a purchase: the worker can only recognize it after finality reaches that timestamp. In the live ROBIN369 test, finality took approximately 15–20 minutes, adding that delay before recognition. Ordinary transfers carry tracked purchase cost and previous relief, with a new five-minute wait for the received amount. Existing mature holdings retain their age. The deepest-percentage-loss allocation policy, 30-minute finalized price average, minimum aggregate harvest and batch amounts, and gas caps remain unchanged.

The verified launch block timestamp anchors the schedule. The first interval is due 15 minutes after launch, then every 15 minutes. A payment additionally requires 30 minutes of finalized price history. At the 15–20-minute finality delay observed in the live test, that history becomes available approximately 45–50 minutes after launch. Scheduling, funding and eligibility can delay the first payment further. Empty or underfunded intervals retain the treasury for future rounds. Downtime does not create a queue of backdated payouts.

The finalized pipeline overlaps confirmation waits rather than blocking the entire service until each transaction finalizes. Accounting and spendable funds still come from finalized state. Transactions that have been mined but are not yet finalized remain durably tracked; their reward amounts and compensation are reserved before a later round can be prepared. These reservations prevent the same loss or treasury amount from being paid again while the finalized ledger catches up. The in-flight bound pauses new work when confirmations fall behind. Up to 100 recipients fit in a batch, reduced when required by the existing gas cap. Journaled transactions, reservations and round progress survive restarts; no restart refills the gas budget.

Thirty-second polling continues so transactions can be reconciled promptly; it is not the payment schedule. Deferred payment retries keep their separate existing rules. The 15-minute interval is a target for eligible, funded work, not a guarantee of exact arrival or final settlement. Treasury funding, minimum batch size, transaction costs, recipient count, confirmation backlog, RPC availability and successful operation still determine delivery.

The version 4 local launch manifest binds the confirmation mode, eight-transaction limit, 300/900 timing policy and exact worker revision. Historical v1/v2/v3 manifests retain their previous validation, hashes and serial confirmation behavior. In particular, the historical v3 worker waits for harvest finality, payout finality and then the next interval; its 900-second setting does not promise payments every 15 minutes. A previous review or host acknowledgement does not authorize v4. Production remains inactive until the exact launch and matching activation evidence verify. Existing operator journals are never deleted or reset to change a policy.
